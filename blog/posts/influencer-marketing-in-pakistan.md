It passed 11 million views, a second film for the same brand passed 1 million, and both kept working as ads long after the first post.

Apex Solar sells lithium batteries from 1300W to 16kW, and it is our longest-running client, two years and counting. ## Quick answers

### Does influencer marketing work in Pakistan?

Yes, when the creator fits the buyer and the film is built to sell, not just to entertain. Our Apex Solar creator film passed 11 million views and the account's WhatsApp lead ads started 22,800 conversations with dealers and customers at about $0.04 each. Views alone do not pay the bills, so plan the path from the film to a chat before you book anyone.

### Should the creator shoot the video or should the brand?

For a product people need to trust, we shoot it. Our crew films with the creator, so the battery is lit properly, the claims are on screen and the cut can run as an ad. The creator brings the voice and the audience. We bring the camera, the script and the edit.

### Big celebrity or smaller creator?

Pick by audience, not follower count. Apex has worked with names like Shahid Khan and Shaheen Shah Afridi and with Dr Sab. The film that reached 11M views was not the most glamorous idea. It was the one closest to the buyer's daily problem.

## What the 11M-view film actually shows

Here is the film in one sentence: a presenter in a waistcoat walks through a neighbourhood shop, full of crisps, rusk and soft drinks, and talks about the one thing every shopkeeper in Pakistan worries about. The power going off.

It runs for under a minute. Watch it frame by frame and you can see every decision we made:

- **The setting is the buyer's world.** A kiryana shop with a fridge, lights and a till. Every shopkeeper watching sees their own counter. Nobody needs to imagine where a battery fits.
- **The hook is one word.** Early in the film "WAPDA" fills the screen in huge red letters behind the presenter. In Pakistan that word carries load-shedding, bills and frustration in five letters. You do not need sound to understand it.
- **The product arrives as proof.** When the battery appears, the facts sit on screen beside it: Pakistan's No 1, 16 kWh capacity, 6 years warranty. Specs land as an answer to the problem, not as a sales sheet.
- **Trust before the ask.** A "Nationwide support network" card lists Apex locations across Pakistan. For a big purchase, the question after "does it work?" is "who fixes it?". The film answers both.
- **It ends on the pain, in Urdu.** The closing frames put two questions on screen: bijli ka bill? load-shedding? The viewer leaves with the problem fresh and the brand attached to it.

None of that is luck. It came from a brief written before anyone picked up a camera. You can watch the film on the [Apex Solar case study](/work/apex-solar.html).

## Influencer marketing in Pakistan: why most campaigns fade

Before the process, the honest diagnosis. When creator campaigns in Pakistan underdeliver, it is rarely because the creator was bad. It is usually one of these:

**The brief is "make something about our product".** The creator guesses, and the guess is about the creator, not the buyer.

**The product looks cheap.** Phone footage in poor light makes an expensive battery look like a toy. For a high-ticket product, the shot itself is a trust signal.

**No usage rights.** The film does well organically, the brand wants to run it as an ad, and nobody agreed that in the contract. The best asset of the campaign sits unused.

**No path to a sale.** Viewers love it, then have nowhere to go. No WhatsApp number on screen, no ad pointing to a chat, no one ready to answer.

**One and done.** The film posts once on the creator's page and the brand never sees it again.

Every step in our process is there to fix one of those.

## Step by step: what we do on a creator shoot

### 1. Shortlist by audience, not fame

A creator with millions of followers who mostly reaches teenagers is the wrong pick for a 16kW battery. We look at who actually watches, what they comment on, and whether the creator can talk about the product without it feeling forced. For Apex, the buyers are homeowners, shopkeepers, dealers and installers. That shaped every choice.

### 2. Write the hook with the creator

The script is built around the first three seconds. For the 11M film it was the WAPDA moment. We agree the hook, the one main claim and the call to action before the shoot day, and we write it in the creator's own voice so it never sounds like an ad read.

### 3. Shoot it ourselves

This is the step most influencer marketing in Pakistan skips. Our crew brings the camera, lights and sound, and we have run shoot days in Multan and Karachi. The creator does what they are great at. We make sure the product, the text and the brand are clean enough to run on Meta for months. Our [video production](/video-production-company/) page covers how we plan and cut films like this.

### 4. Lock the rights early

Fees, deliverables and ad usage are agreed up front. That one line in the contract is what let the Apex films keep working after the creator's own post went quiet.

### 5. Put money behind the winner

Once a film proves itself organically, it runs as ads from the brand's account to the buyers who matter. One Apex creator film ad drew 7.1M video views on its own in Ads Manager. The lead ads point to WhatsApp, because in Pakistan a battery sale starts with a chat, not a checkout. That is how the account reached 22,800 conversations at about $0.04 each. More on that side is on our [Meta ads](/meta-ads-agency/) page.

### 6. Report what matters

Views are the headline. Conversations and cost per result are the business. Our report puts both on one page so the brand can see which film, which creator and which cut actually brought enquiries.

## The second film: teach instead of sell

For the second Apex creator film we went the opposite way. Instead of a shop and a pain point, it is an explainer. The presenter opens in a cluttered workshop with "Gaston Planté" written on the wall, the inventor of the lead-acid battery, walks the viewer through the old routine of topping up acid batteries by hand, and takes us to an Apex service centre in Karachi and the Apex 5220 Pro with faster charging.

It passed 1 million views. Different format, same principle: answer the real question in the buyer's head. Many solar buyers in Pakistan still run lead-acid batteries and are not sure lithium is worth the jump. A film that explains the jump honestly keeps working for months, because that question does not go away. We cover more of this thinking in our post on [solar company marketing in Pakistan](/blog/solar-company-marketing-in-pakistan/).

## Carry the creator into the real world

Creator content works hardest next to real moments. For Apex we carried the campaign into three solar expos, in Lahore, Islamabad and Karachi, with invitations, booth numbers, an installer lucky draw and live highlights from the floor. The point is that someone who saw a film online and then walks past the stand recognises the same brand. The design system we built for Apex keeps the films, the posters and the booth speaking one language.

## Influencer marketing in Pakistan: what to take away

- **Start from the buyer's daily problem.** The 11M film is about the power going off in a shop, not about a battery.
- **Win the first three seconds without sound.** One big word on screen did more than any voiceover.
- **Make the specs the proof.** Capacity, warranty and support network appear as answers, not a list.
- **Shoot it properly.** A high-ticket product needs a crew, light and an ad-ready cut.
- **Write ad rights into the deal.** Your best film should run for months, not once.
- **Point it at a conversation.** WhatsApp lead ads turned attention into 22,800 chats at about $0.04 each.
- **Use more than one format.** A pain point film and an explainer reach different buyers.
