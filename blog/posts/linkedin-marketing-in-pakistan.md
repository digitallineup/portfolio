## Quick answers

### Does LinkedIn marketing work for B2B companies in Pakistan?

Yes, when the page teaches buyers something. Procurement managers, brand managers and exporters use LinkedIn to check whether a supplier is serious. A page full of useful explainers, certifications and proof from the factory floor does that job before your sales team even calls. A page full of greetings does not.

### What should a manufacturer post on LinkedIn?

Post the buyer's problem first and your machine second. The formats that work for us are myth versus fact posts, process explainers, certification proof, quality control content, interactive quizzes, short factory films and carousels that teach one useful idea. Keep national days and greetings, but make them a small share of the calendar.

### Are LinkedIn carousels still worth making for B2B?

Yes. A carousel lets you explain a process step by step, and every swipe keeps the buyer with your brand a little longer. Our ten-slide B2B playbook carousel for Rainbow Printing is built exactly that way: one hook, one idea per slide, and a clear ask at the end.

## Why most B2B company pages in Pakistan get ignored

The problem is not the platform. It is that most pages are written for the owner, not the buyer.

The owner wants to show the new machine, the certificate on the wall and the team photo from the annual dinner. All of that is fine, but on its own it answers a question nobody asked. A brand manager at a snack company is thinking about why her chips go stale before the expiry date, why the red on her pack looks different from batch to batch, and why her last print job got delayed at prepress. If your page answers those questions, she follows you. If it shows her the same machine photo you posted in 2019, she scrolls past.

That is the line we used on the playbook carousel for Rainbow: "B2B is boring is a lie. Your posts are boring."

## LinkedIn marketing in Pakistan: what we built for Rainbow Printing

Rainbow Printing Solutions makes flexible packaging: the pouches and films that snacks, rice, cooking oil and frozen food come in. Here is the content mix we built for their LinkedIn and Instagram, with real examples from the feed.

**Myth versus fact posts.** One post takes the common belief that "rotogravure printing is only for big orders" and answers it: expensive to start, economical to scale. It corrects a real objection a buyer might have before they ever send an enquiry.

**Process and quality explainers.** We designed posts on oxygen transmission (OTR) and water vapour transmission (WVTR) testing in their QC lab, showing the actual testing machines and explaining in one line why each test protects the product inside the pack. Another post shows a five-point quality check before dispatch: colour match, lamination bond, die cut registration, material integrity and a packaging audit.

**Mistakes buyers make.** "Why packaging projects fail before printing starts" lists three avoidable problems: a wrong dieline, low resolution artwork and RGB files instead of CMYK. That post is useful to a designer at a client company even if they never buy from Rainbow, which is exactly why it gets saved and shared.

**Choosing the right packaging.** A carousel matches product types to priorities: grease resistance for oily products, a moisture barrier for export goods like basmati rice, and low temperature seal integrity for frozen food.

**Proof, not claims.** Certification posts for international Halal certification and SGS FSSC 22000, a post on their solar operated facility shot from the air, and a numbers post: 250+ employees and 25+ years of industry experience. Each one is designed like a premium product ad, not a scanned certificate.

**Interactive posts.** "One of these is your brand red." Two red squares, A and B, and a request to comment the answer before the reveal. The point underneath it is serious: colour is not matched by eye, it is measured, and Rainbow uses X-Rite technology to do it.

**Speed you can feel.** "While you read this, we laminated 40 meters." The small print explains it: their Nordmeccanica line runs at up to 400 metres a minute. It turns a spec sheet number into something a buyer actually remembers.

**Borrowed stories.** A post shows how one famous chips pack evolved from 2003 to 2026, with the line "Market leaders don't age. They evolve." Another compares how packaging sells in Japan, the USA, Europe and Pakistan. Nobody knows your spec sheet, but everybody knows Lay's.

**Culture and events.** National days, Basant and Father's Day still appear, but in the same premium visual language. We also branded their internal cricket tournament, the Rainbow Champion League 2026, because a company that looks after its people is a supplier buyers trust.

## How we plan a month of B2B LinkedIn content

A B2B calendar is not a restaurant calendar. Buyers make slower, bigger decisions, so the feed has to build trust over months, not sell in one post. This is roughly how we balance it.

**Teach first.** Around half the month goes to explainers, myths, mistakes and "how to choose" carousels. These are the posts that get saved, and saves tell the platform your content is worth showing to more people.

**Prove second.** Certifications, lab tests, machines, capacity and team. Proof posts are what a procurement team checks before they shortlist you.

**Engage third.** Quizzes, A or B questions, and "comment your answer" posts. They are easy to make and they start conversations in the comments, which is where a sales team can follow up.

**Celebrate last.** National days and festivals, done well and kept short. They show the company is alive, but they should never be the whole feed.

Every post carries the same visual system: the brand red, a black or clean white base, and one clear headline. When someone lands on the page, the grid looks like one serious company, which matters a lot when the buyer is an export client who will never visit your factory in person.

## Mistakes I see on Pakistani B2B pages

**Only posting greetings.** If eight of your last ten posts are greetings, a buyer cannot tell what you make.

**Writing for your own team.** Internal jargon, model numbers and machine names mean nothing to a brand manager. Lead with the outcome she cares about.

**Stock photos of factories that are not yours.** Buyers can tell. Your real floor, your real lab and your real people are more convincing, even if the lighting is not perfect. Better still, get them shot properly.

**No video.** A ten second clip of a press running or a pouch being sealed does more than three paragraphs about capacity. That is why Rainbow's feed includes two motion films, one of them a 3D particles film that makes printing look cinematic.

**Treating the page as a noticeboard.** A post with no point of view gets no reaction. Every post should teach, prove or ask something.

## What to take away

- **LinkedIn works for B2B in Pakistan** when the page teaches buyers something useful.
- **Lead with the buyer's problem**, then show the machine or the certificate that solves it.
- **Make proof look premium.** A certificate designed like an ad gets more attention than a scan.
- **Use carousels to teach one idea** with a hook, one point per slide and a clear ask.
- **Film the factory.** Your production floor is the content your competitors are not posting.
