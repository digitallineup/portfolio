Walk past any electronics store in Multan and read the signs. Samsung, LG, TCL, Haier, Dawlance, Orient. Every shop sells the same fridges, the same ACs and the same TVs, often at nearly the same price. The only thing that is really yours is the name over the door. That is the problem brand mascot design in Pakistan is best at solving: when the product is identical, the brand has to become a character people remember.

Shan Electronics is a Multan electronics store that wanted to feel like a national brand. Our studio built the identity, gave it a face with the Shan mascot, and has run the daily feed since: the store opening, summer heat campaigns, wedding season packages and every product drop. This post walks through how we designed and used that mascot, what it did well, and where we deliberately kept it out of the picture.

## Quick answers

### Does a local retail store need a brand mascot?

Not always, but a store selling the same brands as every shop on the street gains a lot from one. A mascot gives people something to recognise in the feed before they read a word, and it can carry teasers, offers and seasonal posts without the brand starting from zero each time.

### Is a 2D or a 3D mascot better for social media?

For Shan we chose 3D, because a rendered character can stand in real, recognisable places like a flyover or a city landmark and look like it belongs there. A flat 2D character is cheaper to pose and great for illustrated posts. Pick the one that suits where your campaigns will live.

### How do you use a mascot in marketing without overusing it?

Give it jobs, not wallpaper duty. The Shan mascot fronts teasers, store opening moments and campaigns that need personality. Price posts and product drops run on the product and the store front instead, so the character stays special when it appears.

## Brand mascot design in Pakistan starts with the brand, not the cartoon

Search for mascot design in Pakistan and most of what comes up is marketplace gigs: a cute character, delivered in a few days, ready to paste next to any logo. That is the wrong order. A mascot that was not designed with the identity looks like clip art the moment it sits beside your sign.

So with Shan we built the identity first. The mark is a lightning S, drawn from two ideas at once: the letter S and an electric sign. The palette is a bright lime on charcoal, which glows on a store front at night and cuts through a busy feed in the day. We carried it onto the shop front, the staff polo shirts and a brand book, and wrote a short line for the bottom of every post: "Shan sy kharido", buy from Shan.

Only then did we design the character. He wears a green hoodie and a green cap with the S mark on the front, so even in a crop where you cannot see the logo, you know whose mascot he is. He is friendly, a little cheeky and clearly local, which is the tone the whole feed speaks in. You can see the mark, the store front and the full brand book on the [Shan Electronics case study](/work/shan-electronics.html), and how mascots fit into a complete identity in our guide to [brand identity design in Pakistan](/blog/brand-identity-design-in-pakistan/).

## The store opening: a mascot that gave directions

The mascot's first big job was the opening of Shan's new branch on Model Town Road, Multan. A store opening is a tricky thing to market. You want people talking about it before the shutters go up, but "coming soon" posts are easy to ignore.

We ran it as a teaser campaign in two layers.

**First, the banners.** A set of posts showed workers hanging a lime banner from a tall building, each with a different line in Roman Urdu. The first read "Tower se elaan ho gaya, Model Town mein Shan aa raha hai": the announcement has gone out from the tower, Shan is coming to Model Town. The others teased wedding shopping and better prices. Nobody had seen the store yet, but they had seen the colour.

**Then, the mascot tour.** The Shan mascot appeared, life size and rendered in 3D, at Multan landmarks people drive past every week:

- **Northern Bypass**, pointing down the road: "ab bas mudna baqi hai", now there is just one turn left.
- **Model Town**, leaning on the landmark arch: "hum aa rahay hain", we are coming.
- **Nagana Chowk**, resting an arm on the dome: "se Shan ab door nahi", Shan is not far from here.

Each post works on its own, and together they read like a countdown with directions. The trick is local knowledge. A post that names a real chowk tells people in that part of the city that this is their store, and it gives them something to comment and share ("I pass that every day"). That is something a stock character on a white background cannot do.

## Where we kept the mascot out

Here is the part most brands get wrong once they have a mascot: they put it on everything. Within a month the character is wallpaper, and nobody notices it any more.

For Shan, the mascot is used for moments that need personality: teasers, openings and announcements. The everyday selling runs on two other systems.

**The store as the hero.** For product drops, we placed the product itself, oversized, on top of the Shan store front: a microwave asking "Khana garam karne mein itna waqt?", a washing machine asking if you are still washing clothes like it is 2005, a QLED TV, a gas hob and a kitchen hood each with its own teaser and launch post. Every one of these posts quietly advertises the building too, so people learn what the store looks like before they visit.

**Multan's own heat.** Summer campaigns are built around the city's real weather. One post shows a Multan street crowd staring at the sky under the line "Multan's situation right now", another simply says "Multan needs cooling. We have it." AC posts carry the model and price in a clean layout, because when someone is shopping for an AC in June they want facts, not a cartoon. We cover how to shoot and lay out these posts in our guide to [product photography in Pakistan](/blog/product-photography-in-pakistan/).

**A second cast for weddings.** For wedding season we did not stretch the mascot into a groom costume. We drew a new illustrated bride and groom for the #ShadiKaMosam campaign, which carried three "Sasta Shadi" packages, each listing the appliances included with the old and the new price. Different season, different characters, same lime and charcoal system underneath.

## Films: the question every shop owner asks

Alongside the posters we made three films for Shan: a brand story film, a branding reel and a store reel. The brand story film opens on the line "You sell", then flicks through the big appliance brand names, the same names on every sign in the market. The answer lands a few seconds later: "Unless your shop is a brand too." From there it walks through the mark, the colour, the type, the Roman Urdu voice, the uniforms and the store front, where "the shop becomes the billboard".

The branding reel starts with a question typed into a search bar, the one every shop owner asks: why does nobody remember my shop? It is the problem from the top of this post, told in under a minute.

Films like this matter for retail because they explain the identity to the people who matter most early on: the owner's own team, suppliers and repeat customers who notice the change. The [video production](/video-production-company/) page has more on how we plan and cut short brand films.

## Mistakes to avoid with a brand mascot

**Buying the character before the brand.** A template mascot with your logo pasted on its shirt rarely survives a second campaign. Design it inside the identity.

**Using it on every post.** Overuse kills recognition. Give the mascot specific jobs and let product and price posts carry the rest.

**Placing it nowhere.** A mascot floating on a gradient says nothing. Put it in places your buyers know: a flyover, a chowk, your own shop front.

**No rules.** Without poses, expressions and do's and don'ts in the brand book, the second designer will draw a different character. Shan's identity lives in one brand book for exactly this reason.

**Forgetting to sell.** Every Shan post ends with the store, the brand line or a clear offer. A charming mascot that never points to a product is a hobby, not marketing.

## Brand mascot design in Pakistan: what to take away

- **Solve the sameness problem.** If you sell the same brands as your neighbours, a character can be the difference people remember.
- **Design the identity first.** The mascot should wear your mark, your colours and your tone.
- **Go local.** Real landmarks and real weather made Shan's posts feel like Multan, not a template.
- **Give the mascot jobs.** Teasers and announcements, not every price post.
- **Build a system around it.** Store front posts, price layouts and seasonal characters keep the feed fresh while the mascot stays special.
