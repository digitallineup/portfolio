A new restaurant gets one opening. On that day the sign, the menu, the staff and the first takeaway bag all tell people what kind of place this is, long before anyone tastes the food. Most restaurant branding in Pakistan is still done backwards: a quick logo, a printer's stock menu, plain shopping bags and an Instagram page that looks like a different restaurant. Customers notice, even if they could not tell you why.

Spice & Grill came to our studio before opening with a clear brief: an identity that feels warm and appetising and works everywhere a customer meets it, from the sign outside to the takeaway bag. This post walks through exactly what we built, the decisions behind each piece, and the restaurant branding ideas you can take into your own launch, whether you run a grill, a café or a family dining hall.

## Quick answers

### What does restaurant branding include?

Restaurant branding covers the logo and its lockups, a colour palette, typefaces, the menu design, signage, staff uniforms, cups and tableware, takeaway packaging, social media templates and a brand book that ties it all together. For Spice & Grill we designed every one of these before opening day.

### How long does restaurant branding take in Pakistan?

Most of our identities take two to four weeks from the first brief to the finished brand book. Printing signs, packaging and uniforms comes after that, so start at least a month or two before your opening date.

### What colours work best for a restaurant logo?

The best colours are the ones your competitors are not using. Many grills and fast food places in Pakistan reach for red and yellow, so a red logo disappears on the street. Spice & Grill uses olive, charcoal and cream, which feels fresh and warm and stands out next to every red sign around it.

## Restaurant branding in Pakistan starts on the street, not on a screen

Before we drew anything, we listed every place a customer would meet Spice & Grill. The hanging sign outside. The menu on the table. The apron on the person taking the order. The coffee cup in someone's hand. The takeaway box on a desk at work the next day. And the Instagram grid where a friend sees that box.

That list is the real brief. A logo that only has to look good on a white screen is easy. A logo that has to read from across a road on a square sign, print cleanly on a paper sleeve and still look sharp embroidered on a polo shirt is a much tighter problem, and it rules out a lot of fussy ideas early.

## The mark: a flame that cooks something

Plenty of grill logos use a flame. The problem is that a flame on its own says "fire", not "food". So the Spice & Grill symbol is a flame rising out of a frying pan, which reads as cooking in a single glance. In the main wordmark, a small version of that flame sits on top of the "i" in Spice, so the name and the symbol always travel together.

From that one idea we built a family of lockups in the brand book:

- **The stacked wordmark**, "Spice & Grill" on two lines, for the sign, the bags and the menu cover.
- **A horizontal lockup** with the full flame and pan, plus "ESTD 2026", for the letterhead, the website header and wide spaces.
- **A round badge** with the name around the edge and "Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner" underneath, made for stickers, cup sleeves and stamps.
- **The flame and pan alone**, for the smallest uses, like a mug or the corner of a letterhead.

Four lockups sounds like a lot until you try to put a two-line wordmark on a round sticker. Every restaurant needs a long version, a compact version and a symbol it can use on its own.

## Colour and type: why we skipped red

The palette is three colours: olive green (#8a996e), a warm charcoal (#2f2b25) and cream (#f8f2eb). Olive feels fresh and natural, a little herb-like, which suits a menu built on spice. Charcoal nods to the grill without shouting. Cream keeps everything warm and makes the food photography look good next to it.

The type pairing does the same job. **Laviossa**, a soft, high-contrast serif, carries the logo and headlines and gives the brand its friendly, slightly upmarket feel. **Poppins**, a clean sans serif, handles prices, menu items and small print, where reading quickly matters more than personality.

Writing the exact codes into the brand book matters more than most owners think. "Our green" turns into five different greens across a sign maker, a packaging printer and a uniform supplier. A hex code and a font name do not.

## A menu system, not a menu

Restaurants change their menus all the time: new dishes, new prices, a breakfast menu, a Ramadan menu. If every change means a new design, the menu slowly drifts away from the brand.

So we built Spice & Grill a menu system. One layout, with clear sections for breakfast, lunch, dinner, mains and specials, printed in three colourways: cream, olive and charcoal. A large faded flame sits behind the text as a quiet pattern. The owner can swap dishes and prices without calling a designer, and a seasonal menu can switch colourway so regulars notice something is new.

## Takeaway packaging is your cheapest advert

In Pakistan, a lot of restaurant food leaves the building, by delivery rider or in the customer's hand. Every one of those bags is a small advert that walks into offices, homes and Instagram stories. Plain bags waste that.

For Spice & Grill we kept the packaging simple enough to print affordably. The boxes are standard kraft containers with clear lids, wrapped in a single olive band that carries the logo. That band is the clever part: the restaurant buys ordinary boxes and only prints the band, so the branding cost stays low while every order looks finished. The paper bags come in olive and cream, each with the logo, "All you can eat here" and "Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner", so the bag tells people what the place serves as well as its name.

## Staff are part of the brand

Your team is the most visible part of your restaurant. Aprons, polo shirts and caps are seen by every customer, every day, and they appear in the background of almost every photo people take inside.

We designed olive aprons with the cream wordmark, and olive polo shirts with charcoal collars and cuffs, the logo on the chest and a larger one on the back. Paired with branded mugs and cups, the inside of the restaurant looks like one place rather than a collection of supplier freebies.

## Launching the brand: a film, not just a post

A new identity deserves a moment. For Spice & Grill we cut a short film that reveals the mark: the flame lines animating in, the type settling, the menu coming to life. It opens with one line that sums up the whole project: "Your restaurant opens once." The film gives the restaurant something to post on launch day, pin to the top of its profile and use in its first ads. You can watch it on the [Spice & Grill case study](/work/spice-and-grill.html), alongside the full brand book.

## What comes after the identity

A brand book is the start, not the finish. Once the sign is up, the identity has to live in the feed every week: food photography that matches the palette, short films, festival posts and deals. That is where most restaurants either keep their brand or lose it.

We see the payoff of that consistency with the Shangrilla group, where we run social media, food photography and campaigns for the bakery, the Chinese restaurant and the garden BBQ. Their 27 Meta campaigns of food films and deal creatives reached 3.7M people and earned more than 2M post engagements, with every post in the same look and every film showing the real food. Our post on [restaurant and bakery marketing in Multan](/blog/restaurant-and-bakery-marketing-in-multan/) breaks that down in full.

## Restaurant branding mistakes we see again and again

**Copying the category colour.** Red and yellow are taken. Pick a palette that stands out on your street, then use it everywhere.

**Approving the logo on a white page.** Ask to see it on the sign, the bag, the apron and a phone screen first. We show every concept on mockups like these before a client chooses.

**Designing one menu instead of a system.** If changing a price means calling a designer, the menu will drift within months.

**Forgetting takeaway.** If half your orders leave in plain bags, half your brand exposure is going to waste.

**Opening without content.** The sign goes up and the Instagram page is empty. Plan the logo film and the first month of posts alongside the identity.

## Restaurant branding ideas to take away

- **List every touchpoint first.** Sign, menu, staff, cups, packaging and feed. That list is your real brief.
- **Make the mark mean food.** A symbol that suggests cooking, not just fire or a fork, is easier to remember.
- **Choose colours your street is not using.** Then lock them down with exact codes.
- **Build a menu system.** One layout, several colourways, easy to update.
- **Brand the cheap parts cleverly.** A printed band on a stock box gives you branded takeaway for a fraction of the cost of custom boxes.
- **Launch with a moment.** A short logo film and a planned first month give people a reason to notice.
